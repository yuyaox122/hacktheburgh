## Round-1 synthesis (before the 3 refused judges were re-run and Ian's 5 angles were added)

# Decap angles: final decision report

## 1. Is there a clear winner?

**No.** Three angles are effectively tied:

- **Promised vs Proven** (G-Research, 82)
- **Green Tests, Wrong Chip** (MetaView, 81)
- **Poisoned PR** (MetaView, 80)

These are average scores out of 100 from the simulated judges. The three are within 2 points of each other, and no judge called a clear winner except one JetBrains engineer, and only for that one track. The red team then knocked all three down to about 61 to 72. Two red-team reviewers each looked at every angle. Of the six reviews of the top three, only one said "this survives if you fix it". The biggest single problem: the best story we have ("the datasheet promises 8 microphones, the chip has 2") is a copy-pasted settings file. That chip was never a microphone chip.

Three quick tests decide between the contenders:

- **Hour 1:** can we run the chip authors' own tests (written with cocotb, a test tool for chips) on our Mac? Green Tests depends on this.
- **Hour 2:** does an AI code reviewer miss the sneaky changes? Poisoned PR depends on this.
- **Hour 2:** can we find even one dead pin that only the gate wiring shows and the source code does not? Promised vs Proven depends on this.

All three also depend on one shared test by **hour 6**: proving chips that have counters or state machines. That works on 1 of 3 tries today.

## 5. Angles that look strong but should be dropped

- **Pick a Number and Hide and Seek** (wow 22 and 21): the best card-flip moments, but on their own judges say "you planted it yourselves". Use the trick as the finale of the winning angle, not as the angle itself.
- **GlassDie x Decap atlas** (wow 22): really two products to build. Only about 20 of the roughly 375 chips are small enough to read back. We have no wire data, and the team is 3 people, not 4.
- **Shipped vs Reviewed**: the red team actually ran it. About 3 of 9 chips proved, one gave a self-contradicting answer, and the repo-drift census found one chip that missed its deadline and **zero** shipped bugs. The likely headline is "everything matches".
- **Pre-flight**: there is no public record of which chips came back broken, so "would have caught it" can't be shown. A CI bot comment also takes minutes and looks like any other bot.
- **View Source, The Layout Doesn't Lie, Will It Work?**: rated 2 out of 5 for feasibility in 16 hours, or no reliable labels to test against.

## 6. Against the anchors (GlassDie 90.7, Fault Lines 89.3)

**No Decap angle beats our other ideas.** The best raw score (82) is 9 points below GlassDie. After the red team, the best realistic score is about 66 to 72, which is roughly 20 points below.

One caveat in Decap's favour: GlassDie's 90.7 came after five rounds of rewriting against the same simulated judges and was never red-teamed this way, so the true gap is probably smaller. Even so, nothing here shows Decap ahead.

If the team stays on Decap, go with **Green Tests plus the judge's-number finale** for MetaView and JetBrains. Switch to Promised vs Proven only if the hour-2 search finds a feature that synthesis really deleted. If the hour-6 test on counter and state-machine chips fails, there is no strong reason to choose Decap over Fault Lines or GlassDie.
