# Decap Witness, revised to survive the red team

## 1. The idea and the one job

We put a real Tiny Tapeout chip on screen in 3D. AI agents dropped onto a block write short statements about what it does, for example "this part adds the two input nibbles". A statement can colour the silicon only after Yosys proves it against the chip's own netlist; a wrong guess comes back with the exact input that breaks it.

**The one job:** explain a block of a chip you did not design, in statements short enough to read, every one of them proven. The user is someone who inherited a Tiny Tapeout or student chip, or a reviewer handed netlist-only IP. Our decompiler already produces proven Verilog for the whole chip, but on the hard parts that Verilog is a 50-cell blob (`bit0 <= (~((~state0[10] & ui_in[3]) | …`). Witness turns that blob into one line, proven true.

**What changed after the red team:**
- **Cut** the sabotage, the phone page, arm C, the vacuity badge and multi-agent locks.
- **Fixed** the proof setup, the coverage rule, the register names and the demo script.
- **Moved** the judge's moment onto chips where it can't break.

## 2. How the agents work (the new part)

```
 judge drags token onto a block
            |
   REGION AGENT (Claude, Ian's loop, reuses #21 region scope)
     get_region -> simulate / probe_truth (look, free)
     -> propose_claim("count' == count + 1 when ui_in[3]")   <- FIRST DRAFT logged
            |
   CHECKER (no LLM: core/claims.py + Yosys)
     parse; names must be ports or flop ids
     assume rst_n=1, ena=1
     sat -seq 1 -prove   (registers free)
     tautology check (claim true of ANY chip? -> reject)
       |                         |
     PROVEN                   REFUTED + counterexample
       |                         |  BMC 30 cycles from reset:
   credit rule:                  |  also wrong -> "semantic error"
   labelled shape                |  holds      -> "true only from reset"
   (adder, counter, mux, ...)    v
   OR <= 1/3 the literals     back to agent, refine (max 2)
   of the decompile cone
       |
   cells leave the fog; English rendered BY CODE from the formal line
```

The rules:
- **The LLM proposes and only the Checker colours.**
- **Restating the decompile gets no credit.** A proven claim that just copies the decompile goes into a "restated" bucket and clears no fog. This closes the copy-paste loophole.
- **Registers are keyed by flop cell id** (`q_c41`). The decompiler's names (`count0`) are display labels only, so edits to `decompile.py` can't break cached claims. Every cache key and seal also includes the decompiler commit.
- **Counter ranges and one-hot facts** are tried with `-tempinduct`. If induction succeeds they show green "INDUCTIVE"; otherwise they stay amber "BOUNDED". They are never shown green on a bounded check.
- **One agent at a time.** Results are cached by (netlist hash, decompiler commit, region).

## 3. What the judge sees in 90 seconds

- **0-10 s, the job.** "You inherited this chip. Tiny Tapeout's own Explorer (credited) shows every wire. Our decompiler gives proven Verilog, and here's what that looks like." Show the 50-cell `state0 <= …` blob on kapilan_alarm, a dev chip, labelled "dev: we tuned on this."
- **10-35 s, agents.** We drop an agent token on the blob. Status chips read "probing 256 vectors" and then "proving".
  - The first draft goes red with a counterexample. The agent refines it, it goes green, and that patch leaves the fog.
  - The panel shows the 1-line claim next to the blob, for example "11× shorter, proven".
  - The claim text will be whatever the real tool produced at Gate 1, never written by hand.
- **35-72 s, THE JUDGE'S MOMENT** (on a chip we never tuned on).
  - The judge picks any chip from the printed card: 9 combinational held-out chips, named and frozen at 19:10.
  - They flip the DIP switches for about 10 s and watch the LEDs.
  - Then they type what they think: "LED 3 lights when switches 1 and 2 are on."
  - The formal line and code-written English appear, and the judge clicks [Yes, that's what I meant].
  - If it is **refuted** (about 1 s), they click "load counterexample": the switches flip and the LEDs show the chip's answer beside the ghost prediction.
  - If it is **proven**, the judge's own claim clears that patch of fog: they just explained part of a chip.
  - Either outcome is honest, and we cannot script it.
- **72-85 s, the number card**, sealed before scoring (section 4).
- **85-90 s, close.** "Decap Witness: agents that explain silicon and can't bluff."

## 4. The measured number

The freeze happens at 02:30: git tag `bench-freeze`, and a SHA-256 of {code, prompts, model id, split, decompiler commit} goes on the board before any test chip is scored.

**Held-out n = 15 distinct chips.** That is 18 test chips, minus the 2 micro_tiles chips that fail to load, minus the duplicate: `dpmu` and `dpmunit` have identical netlists, so they count once. 9 are combinational and 7 are Wokwi; the card shows the count for each class.

1. **First-draft error rate.** We log the agent's first claim per region: it has already used the simulator but has not had Checker feedback. We report the % refuted, split into "semantic error" and "true only from reset". We give n claims, n chips and a bootstrap 95% CI clustered by chip. This is the realistic rival: an agent that has a simulator and no proof.
2. **Explained coverage gain.** Of the logic cells the templates could not name, we report the % credited by proven, compressed or labelled claims, plus the median compression ratio. Chips where templates already cleared everything are listed as "no gap".
3. **Honesty counts:** restated claims, timeouts, unparseable claims, failures by chip name. Refuted claims shown to the user: 0, which we state as a design property, not a result.
4. **Live tally:** every judge and rehearsal claim, logged as proven or refuted.

**Decision rule, fixed now:** if the semantic first-draft error is at least 10% with n ≥ 30 claims, it is the headline. Otherwise the headline is item 2.

## 5. Why it is new, and the tracks

**Prior art:** Tiny Tapeout Explorer already does 3D plus live simulation of real TT dies, and we build on it and credit it. Region-pointed AI tools (GDS-3D-VIEWER, PhotonForge, klayout-mcp, Z80 Explorer MCP) explain things nobody checks. Nothing we found makes an agent's statement about a region of a taped-out chip pass a proof before it is shown.

| Track | Opening line |
|---|---|
| **G-Research (primary)** | "On 15 held-out Tiny Tapeout chips frozen before tuning, X% of an AI's first explanations were wrong, and the proof caught every one with an input you can try." |

## 7. Top 3 risks

1. **The agents may not compress anything.** This is the hard part: an LLM has to find a short true description of a 50-cell blob. Gate 1 tests exactly that at 22:30. If it fails, the first-draft error rate still works as a measured number, and the judge's moment needs no agent at all.
2. **Proof-setup artefacts could inflate the refutations.** With registers left free, states the chip can never reach, and the reset itself, can refute true claims. Assuming reset and enable are off, plus the BMC classifier, separates these out, and only semantic errors are headlined. One caveat: this classifier has not been run yet.
3. **Load, a shared checkout, and the LLM.** Scope is cut now, not at 02:00. Each session gets its own worktree, the API key is checked by 20:15, and the demo runs from cache with replay as the backup. Laptop on power, no sleep.
